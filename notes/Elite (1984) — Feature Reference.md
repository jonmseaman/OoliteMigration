# Elite (1984) — Feature Reference

Nov 1, 2026 · @Jon Seaman

## Overview

Elite is an open-ended space trading and combat game by David Braben and Ian Bell. Acornsoft published it for the BBC Micro in September 1984 and for the Acorn Electron later that year. It sold 107,898 copies on the BBC Micro and, by Ian Bell's estimate, about 600,000 across all platforms.

You start docked at Lave with 100 credits and a Cobra Mk III: front pulse laser, three missiles, 7 light years of fuel. Nothing else is set: your combat rating, from Harmless to Elite, is the only measure of progress.

- **Trade**: 17 commodities across 2,048 procedurally generated systems in eight galaxies.
- **Fight**: pirates, police, bounty hunters and Thargoids in 3D wireframe space.
- **Upgrade**: lasers, missiles, ECM, fuel scoops, a bigger cargo bay, docking computers, a galactic hyperdrive.
- **Missions**: a few scripted missions in most versions, more on some ports.

Values come from the BBC Micro versions unless a row or note names another platform. Version names used throughout: BBC cassette, BBC disc, 6502SP (BBC Micro with 6502 Second Processor), Master, Electron, C64, Apple II, Spectrum, CPC (Amstrad), NES and Archimedes. "Disc+" means the BBC disc version and the later 6502 versions (6502SP, C64, Apple II, Master, NES). Sequels: Frontier: Elite II (1993), Frontier: First Encounters (1995), Elite Dangerous (2014).

## Galaxy and procedural generation

All 2,048 systems grow from three 16-bit seeds per system, so the universe costs a few bytes. A system's position, government, economy, tech level, population, productivity, radius, species, name and description all derive from its seeds.

### Seeds and galaxies

- Each system has seeds s0, s1, s2. Galaxy 1 starts at Tibedied: s0 = &5A4A, s1 = &0248, s2 = &B753.
- One "twist" is a Tribonacci step (new s2 = s0 + s1 + s2, mod 65,536). Four twists give the next system; 256 systems make a galaxy.
- The galactic hyperdrive rotates every seed byte left by one bit, so eight jumps bring you back to galaxy 1.

### System attributes

| Attribute | Derived from | Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Position | x = s1 high byte, y = s0 high byte | 0–255 each (the chart halves y) |
| Government | Bits 3–5 of s1 low byte | 8 types (below) |
| Economy | Bits 0–2 of s0 high byte; anarchies and feudal worlds can never be rich | 8 types (below) |
| Tech level | (economy XOR 7) + bits 0–1 of s1 high + government ÷ 2 | Shown as 1–15 |
| Population | tech × 4 + economy + government + 1 | 0.1–7.1 billion |
| Gross productivity | ((economy XOR 7) + 3) × (government + 4) × population × 8 | 96–62,480 M CR |
| Average radius | (bits 0–3 of s2 high + 11) × 256 + s1 high | 2,816–6,911 km |
| Species | Bit 7 of s2 low: Human Colonials, or an alien race | — |
| Planet drawing | Bit 1 of tech level: craters, or a meridian and equator | — |

| Value | Government | Economy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Anarchy | Rich Industrial |
| 1 | Feudal | Average Industrial |

- Richer industrial economies and more stable governments give higher tech, population and productivity. Only the economy feeds the market.
- **Species** are built from four parts: size (Large, Fierce, Small or none), colour (Green, Red, Yellow, Blue, Black, Harmless or none), trait (Slimy, Bug-Eyed, Horned, Bony, Fat, Furry or none) and creature (Rodents, Frogs, Lizards, Lobsters, Birds, Humanoids, Felines, Insects), e.g. Black Furry Felines. The arithmetic rules out some pairs: slimy beings are only rodents, frogs, lizards or lobsters.

### Names and descriptions

- **Names** are three or four two-letter pairs from a 32-entry table (AL LE XE GE ZA CE BI SO US ES AR MA IN DI RE A ER AT EN BE RA LA VE TI ED OR QU AN TE IS RI ON). An empty pair adds nothing, so names run 2–8 letters: Lave is LA-VE, Tibedied is TI-BE-DI-ED.
- **Descriptions** ("goat soup") are a seeded phrase grammar choosing among five options per slot: opener, intensifier, opinion, then a planet feature, a local plant or animal named after the system, the inhabitants' traits, or a product or pastime. Random words fill some slots. Lave's reads "Lave is most famous for its vast rain forests and the Lavian tree grub" (generated by the game).
- Descriptions are missing from the cassette and Electron, shown only when docked on disc, and always shown on 6502SP, Master, C64, Apple II and NES.
- **Special descriptions** in the enhanced versions carry mission hints (Usleri during the Constrictor hunt), a warning about illegal cloning on Teorge (a nod to The Dark Wheel), an advert for Elite II on Arredi and jokes about the game Aviator on Anreer.

### Oddities

- On arrival the planet is placed ahead and the sun behind, at distances drawn from the seeds and the economy.
- The "nearest system" search uses |dx|/2 + |dy|/2, not true distance.
- The short-range chart labels one system per text row, so some systems in range have no name; the Constrictor mission hides its target this way.
- An early cassette build could drop a galactic jump into empty space at the galaxy centre and strand a low-fuel pilot.

## Player ship and cockpit

You always fly a Cobra Mk III: a 20 t hold (35 t with the large cargo bay), 7 LY of fuel, up to four missiles and a laser on any of four views. Flight is pitch and roll only, at speeds of 1–40.

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Speed | 1–40 units per loop; launch speed 12. Only missiles (44) are faster; the Asp Mk II and Cougar match it |
| Turning | Roll ±31 and pitch ±8 rate steps; no yaw |
| Cargo hold | 20 t; 35 t with the Large Cargo Bay |
| Fuel | 7.0 LY |
| Missiles | 3 at start, 4 maximum |
| Laser mounts | Front, rear, left, right |
| Energy | 0–255 in four banks; +1 every 8 loops, +2 with the Extra Energy Unit, +3 with the Naval unit |
| Shields | Fore and aft, 0–255 each; recharge only while energy is at least half |
| Start | Docked at Lave with 100 CR, a clean record and a front pulse laser |

The NPC Cobra Mk III is slower and weaker: speed 28, energy 150, three missiles.

### Damage

- Hits from behind drain the aft shield, others the forward shield. Overflow drains the energy banks, and zero energy is death.
- With shields down, each hit has about a 4.3% chance to destroy one item: a whole cargo type, or the ECM, fuel scoops, energy bomb, energy unit or docking computer ("{item} DESTROYED"). The escape pod is never lost.

### Lasers

- The pulse laser fires five pulses a second; beam and military lasers fire every loop and flicker.
- Each firing adds 8 to laser temperature and costs 1 energy. At 242 the laser locks until it cools, by 1 per loop.
- Only a military laser hurts the Cougar and the mission Constrictor, at a quarter of its power.
- Hitting the space station turns it hostile; it can't be destroyed.

### Missiles, ECM and the energy bomb

- **Missiles**: T arms the next missile; a target in the crosshairs locks it (beep, red indicator); M fires; U unarms. A missile kills its target outright. If one hits a ship or station near you, you take 80 damage; an enemy missile that reaches you does 250.
- **Missile defence**: a target with an ECM has a 6.25% chance per check to fire it while your missile is in flight.
- **ECM**: a 32-loop pulse that drains 1 energy per loop and destroys every missile in flight, yours included.
- **Energy bomb**: kills every ship in range except the station. Thargoids are immune on the C64, Apple II, Master and NES; the Constrictor is vulnerable only on disc. The effect is a flash (cassette, disc), lightning (C64, Apple II, Master) or nothing (Electron, 6502SP).

### How the market works

- **Price** × 10 = (base + (random AND mask) + economy × factor) × 4, with economy 0 (Rich Industrial) to 7 (Poor Agricultural). The sum is 8-bit, so narcotics wrap round to almost nothing.
- **Stock** = (base quantity + (random AND mask) − economy × factor) mod 64, so 0–63 units.
- One random byte per arrival drives every price and stock level in the system. Buying lowers stock; selling never raises it; there is no buy/sell spread.
- A positive factor means cheap and plentiful in industrial systems (computers, luxuries, firearms, machinery). A negative factor means cheap in agricultural ones (food, liquor, furs, textiles). The classic run takes food and liquor to industrial worlds and brings computers back.
- Masks set volatility: narcotics prices swing by up to 48 CR, food by 0.4 CR.

### Contraband and cargo rules

- Each launch adds (slaves + narcotics) × 2 + firearms, in tonnes, to your legal record. 25 t of slaves or narcotics makes you a Fugitive (see Combat, NPCs and the law).
- Goods sold by the tonne share the hold (20 or 35 t). Gold, platinum, gem-stones and alien items take no hold space; each is capped at 199 units.
- The cassette sells item by item, asking for a quantity; disc and later let you buy and sell exact amounts.

### Salvage and mining

- Fuel scoops pick up objects that pass just below the nose. A cargo canister holds one random item from Food to Computers, which can include slaves or narcotics.
- An escape pod scoops as 1 t of Slaves, and a Thargon as 1 t of Alien Items.
- Disc and later: wrecks shed alloy plates (Alloys); a mining-laser kill splits an asteroid into 0–3 splinters (Minerals). Rock hermits (6502SP and Master) are mineable bases.
- With a full hold the canister is destroyed with a failure sound.

## Combat, NPCs and the law

Ships spawn on a timer by role (traders, junk, police, pirates and bounty hunters), and lawless governments draw far more pirates. Each ship's AI holds an aggression level (0–63) and an ECM flag; the disc and later versions add role flags that decide who fights whom.

### Spawning

A spawn check runs every 256 main loops, never while docked, inside the safe zone or in witchspace.

| Roll | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Traders and junk | About 13.7% per check: half traders (Cobra Mk III, Python, Boa or Anaconda), half junk (48% asteroid, 50% boulder, 2% canister) |
| Rock hermit | About 1.6% of junk rolls (6502SP, Master) |
| Police | Chance out of 256 = 2 × contraband score (plus your legal record once police are present); 20 t of slaves is about 31% per check |
| Thargoid or Cougar | About 0.4% per check (6502SP, Master, C64): 96.8% Thargoid, 3.2% Cougar |
| Pirates and hunters | Anarchies always pass; other governments pass about 35% × (8 − government) ÷ 8, so a Feudal world gets \~31% and a Corporate State \~4.4% |
| Pack or lone | About 61% packs (disc and later) |
| Pirate pack | 1–4 ships: Sidewinder \~42%; Mamba, Krait, Gecko \~14% each; Adder, Cobra Mk I, Worm \~4.7%; pirate Cobra Mk III \~1.6% |
| Lone bounty hunter | Pirate Cobra Mk III, Asp Mk II, pirate Python or Fer-de-lance, 25% each |
| Witchspace | Thargoids, each with a Thargon, until four are present |

On the cassette, traders are always Cobra Mk IIIs, packs are Sidewinders and Mambas, and lone ships can be Thargoids.

### Tactics, per ship per turn

1. Missiles home on their target; any active ECM destroys them.
2. A hostile station launches Vipers (4–7 at most, by version); a calm one sometimes launches a Shuttle or Transporter. Rock hermits may launch pirates; 6502SP Anacondas launch Worms or Sidewinders.
3. The ship regains 1 energy.
4. Above half energy it considers lasers; between an eighth and a half, missiles; below an eighth it fires a missile (90%) or ejects an escape pod and drifts (10%).
5. Missile chance per turn = missiles left ÷ 32. Thargoids launch Thargons instead.
6. Lasers hit when the ship's nose lines up within a few degrees at close range; the attacker slows after firing.
7. It closes in or turns away depending on aggression and distance; traders and pods head for the planet.

- Cassette and Electron: aggression alone drives behaviour, so police, hunters and pirates all shoot when they line up.
- A ship you hit turns angry and dives at you. Hitting the station, or an innocent ship inside the safe zone, turns the station hostile.
- Thargons lose their ECM and half their speed once no Thargoid remains.

## Ranks and progression

The combat rating is Elite's only progression: nine ranks from Harmless to Elite, earned purely by kills.

Kills needed to reach each rank. The BBC ladder doubles for five ranks, then leaps to 6,400; the NES matches it from Dangerous up.

| Rank | BBC Micro | NES | Archimedes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harmless | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Mostly Harmless | 8 | 2 | 50 |
| Poor | 16 | 8 | 400 |
| Average | 32 | 24 | 1,000 |
| Above Average | 64 | 44 | 1,500 |
| Competent | 128 | 130 | 2,500 |
| Dangerous | 512 | 512 | 4,500 |
| Deadly | 2,560 | 2,560 | 6,000 |
| Very Deadly | — | — | 8,000 |
| Elite | 6,400 | 6,400 | 9,600 |

- "Right On Commander!" flashes every 256 kills from 256 onwards, past Elite too.
- The BBC Micro, Electron and 6502SP count every kill as one, asteroids and police included, so players farmed Vipers outside stations. The C64, Apple II, Master and NES weight each kill by ship type, from 0.02 for a boulder to 5.33 for the Constrictor or Cougar (see Ships and stations).
- The NES eases the early ranks and redraws the commander's portrait as rank, wealth and legal status change.
- Archimedes adds a Very Deadly rank and congratulates you every 50 and every 250 kills.
- The 16-bit versions use a score instead. On the Amiga a Thargoid or Wolf Mk II is worth 150, an Asp Mk II 110, an Adder, Fer-de-lance or Krait 100, a Viper 50 and an orbit shuttle 3.

## Navigation and travel

Hyperspace jumps of up to 7 LY cost fuel by distance; a one-use galactic hyperdrive moves you to the next galaxy. Inside a system you can jump toward the planet, skim the sun for fuel and dock by hand or by computer.

### Hyperspace

- Pick a target with the chart crosshairs. Distance in tenths of a LY = 4 × √(dx² + (dy/2)²), so a galaxy spans about 102 × 51 LY.
- H starts a 15-tick countdown ("HYPERSPACE - {name}"). It is refused while docked, during a countdown, for the current system, or beyond your fuel ("RANGE?").
- On arrival the market is re-rolled, the planet appears ahead and the sun behind, and your legal record halves. Fuel costs 0.2 CR per 0.1 LY at a station.

### Witchspace misjumps

- Any jump has about a 1% chance (3 in 256) to misjump. On the cassette you can force one: enable the X pause option, then hold CTRL as the countdown ends. The competition code records it.
- You end up in empty space near the target with four Thargoids and their Thargons. The fuel is spent, and the in-system jump is blocked.
- The escape pod is fatal in witchspace on the cassette, works on disc and is disabled on 6502SP and Master. The Electron has no witchspace.

### Galactic hyperdrive

- CTRL-H with the drive fitted. Its countdown is 15 on the BBC Micro and Electron and 2 on later versions. It is used up, and it wipes your legal record.
- You arrive at the system nearest the galaxy's centre point (96, 96):

| Arriving in galaxy | System | Tech level | Government |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Usle | 9 | Confederacy |
| 2 | Ororra | 4 | Confederacy |
| 3 | Xeines | 3 | Anarchy |
| 4 | Soenisti | 4 | Anarchy |
| 5 | Ceinan | 7 | Confederacy |
| 6 | Intisoar | 7 | Confederacy |
| 7 | Beritere | 3 | Anarchy |
| 8 | Cecees | 8 | Anarchy |

### In-system jump (J)

- Moves the planet and sun 65,536 units closer, about 2.7 planet radii; junk nearby comes too.
- It is blocked (low beep) by any ship nearby other than asteroids, pods and canisters, inside the safe zone, in witchspace, or with the planet or sun close ahead.

### Safe zone, docking and launching

- The station orbits two planet radii from the planet's centre. Entering a box two radii around it spawns the station, lights the "S" bulb, swings the compass to the station, blocks the in-system jump and enables the docking computer.
- Manual docking passes if the station isn't hostile, you approach within 26° of straight on, inside a 22° safe cone, with the slot within 33.6° of horizontal. There is no speed check. A failure is a crash, survivable only below speed 5.
- Docking computer: the cassette docks you instantly; disc and later fly the approach and match the station's roll, and can still crash. The C64 and NES play The Blue Danube while it flies.
- Launching runs the tunnel, sets speed 12 with the station behind you, and checks your hold for contraband. Disc and later show a hangar after docking.

### Sun, heat and fuel scooping

- Cabin temperature is 30 in deep space and climbs near the sun; past 255 you die.
- With fuel scoops and temperature above 224 you gain 0.1–0.5 LY per check, more at speed ("FUEL SCOOPS ON").
- Measured in sun radii, heating starts at 2.67, scooping at 1.33 and death at 0.90. The Electron has no sun.

### Planet, scale and escape

- An altitude bar warns as you near the planet; zero altitude is a crash.
- Scale in game units: planet and sun radius 24,576; station orbit 65,536; local bubble radius 57,344; Coriolis width 320; top speed 40 per loop.
- The escape pod docks you at the station in a fresh Cobra Mk III with a full tank. You lose the cargo and the pod, and your record is wiped.

## Presentation, packaging and secrets

Elite fitted 3D wireframe ships with hidden-line removal into 32 KB, shipped in an oversized box with a novella and a 64-page manual, and ran a competition to find the first Elite pilots.

### Graphics and technical tricks

- **Hidden-line removal**: back-face culling with a stored normal per face; only faces pointing at you are drawn.
- **Sun**: one horizontal line per pixel row with a random fringe, so its edge shimmers; white on the BBC, red and yellow on 6502SP and Master, a blue star on the NES.
- **Planets**: a circle with either a crater or a meridian and equator, chosen by bit 1 of the tech level.
- **Explosions**: clouds of random dots around the ship's vertices that grow, then shrink; stored seeds let the same cloud redraw exactly.
- **Split screen**: the BBC runs a 2-colour hi-res mode for space and a 4-colour mode for the dashboard on the same screen.
- **Memory**: procedural generation for the universe; disc overlays that load ship sets on launch; the 6502SP runs the game on the second processor for an 18-ship bubble; logarithm tables speed up the maths on later versions.
- **Loading screen**: title banners over a randomly dotted Saturn.
- **16-bit and Archimedes**: solid-filled, shaded ships. The NES adds a 3D-looking dashboard and a commander portrait that grows dark glasses when you turn criminal.

### Sound and music

- BBC Micro and Electron: sound effects only.
- Commodore 64: The Blue Danube while the docking computer flies (a nod to 2001: A Space Odyssey) and the Elite Theme by Aidan Bell on the title screen, arranged by Julie Dunn.
- NES: Elite Theme, Blue Danube, Game Theme and Assassin's Touch (the last two by David Whittaker), with 29 sound effects.
- BBC Master: C64-style effects with volume control. Atari ST: music by Wally Beben.

### Title screens and demos

### Competitions

- **Acornsoft Elite competition** (monthly, September 1984 to March 1985): send a postcard with your credits and the competition code at Competent or better. Each month's best won £100 of software and an "Elite" emblem; winners went to a national tournament in April 1985.
- **Firebird's Order of Elite** (UK until 1986, plus US and German versions): an Elite poster and insignia, monthly prizes, and for the US winner a trip to the UK.
- **The competition code**: a 4-byte number shown on saving. It packs version flags, a byte of cash, the high byte of the kill tally and a checksum, with a tamper bit and a bit for forced misjumps. Acornsoft decoded it with a secret program.
- No official certificate for reaching Elite is documented.

### Lore

- **The Dark Wheel** (Robert Holdstock, 1984): Alex Ryder hunts the killer of his father Jason, teams up with a fugitive pilot and her alien co-pilot, and learns what the Dark Wheel is. It doubles as a primer on trading and combat. A promised 1985 sequel never appeared.
- **Imprint** (Andy Redman, 1991, in the Elite Plus manual): Rif Hood's revenge story, ending in a battle against the Knights Templar terrorists in the Arazaes system.

### A living world (Archimedes, 1991)

- Ships fight each other: pirate packs defend their patches, traders get attacked, and police and pirates skirmish in the distance while you join in or not.
- Police Vipers fly in formation, hunt lawbreakers and tow disabled ships to the station.
- NPC Cobras hunt pirates to raise their own Elite rating.
- Escorted ships run while you fight the escorts.
- Oolite's populator and priority AI already stage NPC fights and escorts. Towing, formation flying and ranked NPC rivals would be new.
