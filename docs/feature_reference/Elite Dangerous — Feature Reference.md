# Elite Dangerous — Feature Reference

Nov 1, 2026 · @Jon Seaman

## Overview and timeline

Elite Dangerous launched on Windows on 16 December 2014 after a £1.58m Kickstarter, and in November 2026 it is still updated every few weeks. Recent years brought Powerplay 2.0 (2024), system colonisation (2025), Operations (2026) and a new ship roughly every quarter. Since November 2022 only the PC "Live" version gets new content.

## The galaxy

The game models the whole Milky Way at real scale: about 400 billion star systems generated by the Stellar Forge, seeded with about 150,000 real catalogue stars. By June 2026 players had catalogued 195 million systems, about 0.05% of the total.

- Sol sits at the origin. Colonia is about 22,000 ly away, Sagittarius A\* (the central black hole) about 25,900 ly and Beagle Point, on the far rim, about 65,279 ly.
- "The Bubble" is populated human space around Sol. Colonisation added tens of thousands of systems to it in 2025.
- Physicist Kay Ross led the Stellar Forge work, which combines procedural generation with hand-placed real data.

### Permits and locked space

- 28 systems need a permit, among them Sol, Sirius, Ross 128, van Maanen's Star, Vega, Beta Hydri, Alioth, Achenar, Summerland, Facece, Shinrarta Dezhra, Crom, Hors, Terra Mater, Tiliala, Isinor, Jotun and Peregrina.
- Federation and Empire permits come with navy rank (for example Sol at Federal Petty Officer, Achenar at Imperial Squire). Alliance and independent permits come from being allied with the controlling faction, and Shinrarta Dezhra admits Elite pilots.
- Some permits can never be earned, and whole sectors (such as Col 70 Sector) are locked off.

### Nebulae and lore regions

- The Pleiades, Witch Head and Coalsack nebulae hold Thargoid signal sources. Real nebulae such as Orion, the Horsehead, California, Eagle and Crab are modelled too.
- The Formidine Rift region and the Dark Wheel faction carry on the original game's lore, including the legend of Raxxla.

### Fighters, vessels and SRVs

- **Ship-launched fighters** (Horizons, 2016): F63 Condor (Federation), Imperial Fighter (Empire), Taipan and GU-97 (independent), and the Guardian hybrids XG7 Trident, XG8 Javelin and XG9 Lance from the Guardian tech broker. NPC pilots hired at a Crew Lounge fly them for a share of earnings.
- **Nomad** (1 July 2026): a small ship-launched exploration vessel with a Mk II Biological Scanner and fixed pulse lasers, launched from Mk I or Mk II vessel bays.
- **SRV Scarab** (Horizons, December 2015) and **SRV Scorpion**: surface buggies for planetary landings.
- **Rhino** (Vodel, 2 September 2026): a three-seat mining SRV with a planetary deposit scanner, a refinery and deployable mining rigs, launched from a new vehicle bay.

### Carriers, capital ships and megaships

- **Fleet carrier** (Drake-class) and **squadron carrier** (Javelin-class): see Fleet carriers, squadrons and colonisation.
- **Farragut-class battle-cruiser** (Federal Navy) and **Majestic-class interdictor** (Imperial Navy): NPC capital ships about 2 km long, seen in high-intensity conflict zones.
- **Megaships** (since 2.3, April 2017): slow-moving NPC vessels, some dockable, such as the Wells-class carrier, Lowell-class science vessel and Bowman-class research vessel.
- **Thargoid Titans**: see Thargoids and Guardians.

### Customisation

- Paint jobs, ship kits (bolt-on body parts), animated Holo-Kits that overlay any paint, decals (including Owners Club and Powerplay ones) and squadron logos.

### Damage model

- **Types**: thermal (lasers, Gauss, shards), kinetic (cannons, frag, shock cannon), explosive (missiles, torpedoes, mines, flak), absolute (Guardian plasma charger; 60% of plasma accelerators), anti-xeno (AX weapons) and caustic (Thargoid weapons and the Enzyme rack).
- **Shields** resist kinetic by 40% and explosive by 50% but take 20% extra from thermal; excess damage spills to the hull.
- **Hull**: damage × the armour's resistance × min(1, armour piercing ÷ hull hardness). AP 20 against an Anaconda (hardness 65) does only 31%.
- **Modules**: a hit has a roughly 40% chance to penetrate at full hull, rising to 80% near death; a rail gun passes through any ship.
- **Resistances** stack with diminishing returns: compressed above 30%, soft cap 65%, hard cap 75%.
- Every shot draws from the weapons capacitor and adds heat; ammunition can be resynthesised from materials.

## Engineering

Twenty-five ship engineers modify modules with grade 1–5 blueprints paid for in collected materials, plus one experimental effect per module. Thirteen on-foot engineers fit suit and weapon modifications.

### How it works

- Each grade takes one or more rolls, and each roll consumes that grade's materials. Most blueprints trade one stat against another: range for mass, damage for heat.
- Reputation with an engineer unlocks higher grades. Reaching grade 3 plus a third earns a referral to the next engineer.
- **Experimental effects**: one per module, added on top of a blueprint.
- **Remote workshop**: pin one blueprint per engineer and apply it at stations and carriers that offer the service; it earns no reputation and adds no experimentals.
- Update 18.08 (2024) eased unlocks and raised material rewards; since July 2026, Merc Coin from Operations also buys blueprints.

## Flight and ship systems

Ships fly with six degrees of freedom and Flight Assist on by default. Power, heat, fuel and the Frame Shift Drive (FSD) shape every trip, from undocking to the next jump.

### Flight model and throttle

- Ships thrust forward and back, up and down, and sideways, and rotate on all three axes. Lateral and vertical thrusters tighten turns and dodge fire.
- The throttle gauge has a blue zone. New pilots are told to stay in it, and players treat it as the best turn-rate speed.
- Flight Assist counter-thrusts to hold the ship steady (default key F). With it off the ship keeps its momentum and drifts, which guides recommend for dogfights.
- Boost is a short burst of speed paid for from the engine capacitor.

### Power distributor and power plant

- The distributor feeds three capacitors: SYS (shields and systems), ENG (engines) and WEP (weapons). Pilots move pips between them; players describe six pips, at most four in one, resetting to 2/2/2.
- Every shot drains WEP, and an empty WEP capacitor stops the guns. Capacitor size varies by ship; the starter Sidewinder's is small.
- The power plant must cover every powered module. Modules sit in priority groups 1–5, and the lowest groups switch off first when draw exceeds output, for example when hardpoints deploy or the plant is damaged.

### Heat

| Heat level | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 100% | Normal running |
| Over 100% | Overheating warning; modules take heat damage |
| Over 150% | Hull damage (player reports) |

- Stars heat ships at a rate set by star class, so fuel scooping runs hot. Lasers and FSD charging add heat too.
- Silent Running closes the heat vents to hide the ship's heat signature. Heat builds inside and the shields drop.
- Heat sinks dump heat when launched, and players use them as decoys against heat-seeking missiles.

### Damage, canopy and life support

- Each module has its own integrity, and hull damage is logged in 20% steps.
- A module at 0% can be revived with Repair/Reboot on the right-hand panel. It returns at very low integrity, taken from other modules.
- The Auto Field-Maintenance Unit repairs modules in flight using ammunition. Since Horizons it can also seal canopy cracks.
- A breached canopy starts the life-support clock, ending in the ship computer's Oxygen Depleted warning. Players give reserves by rating as E 5 min, D 7.5, C 10, B 15 and A 25.
- Damaged modules malfunction with random reboots, misfires and thruster stutter. Self-destruct is always available.

### Fuel, scooping and jump boosts

| Boost | Jump range | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neutron star jet cone | +300% | Fly through the cone; it can damage a module |
| White dwarf jet cone | +50% | The same, around a white dwarf |
| FSD injection: basic, standard, premium | +25%, +50%, +100% for one jump | Synthesised from materials |
| Guardian FSD Booster | Flat bonus | Optional internal module |

- Fuel scoops work on O, B, A, F, G, K and M stars, which players remember as KGB FOAM. Scoop in supercruise at minimum speed, just outside the exclusion zone.
- Fuel sits in a main tank plus a small reservoir, and the low-fuel warning comes below 25%. Fuel-transfer limpets refuel other ships.
- The galaxy map draws a plotted route solid while fuel lasts and dashed beyond it.
- A neutron-star highway to Colonia took about 150 jumps instead of about 450 (2016–18 guide).

### Supercruise

- Supercruise is FSD-driven faster-than-light flight inside a system. Players give the top speed as 2,001 times light speed.
- To arrive, lock the target and keep the throttle in the blue zone. Players drop to about 75% when time-to-target reads 7 seconds; overshooting means looping round to shed speed.
- Supercruise Assist holds the throttle and drops you at the target. Update 6 taught it to steer away from planets when arriving too fast.
- Supercruise Overcharge (SCO) drives, announced on 10 April 2024, add a faster boost mode. Players report it trades fuel, heat and stability for speed.
- Hardpoints and cargo scoops cannot deploy in supercruise, so a fight there starts with an interdiction.
- Signal sources show a threat level before you drop in. Near a planet the ship enters Orbital Cruise.

### Hyperspace jumps

- Large masses mass-lock the FSD and stop it charging. Each ship has a mass-lock rating; the Python's is 17.
- The FSD cools down after a jump or drop. Players put the charge at about 15 seconds and the cooldown at about 10.
- Jump range comes from the FSD's class and rating against the ship's mass. An unengineered Cobra Mk III managed about 30 ly; engineered ships pass 50 ly.
- The route plotter offers Fastest and Economical routes and records each star's class on the way. Ships arrive pointing at the star.

### Interdiction

- An FSD Interdictor drags a target out of supercruise. The victim steers to keep an escape vector ahead and fill their bar first.
- Submitting at zero throttle ends it at once and the FSD recharges sooner, so many pilots submit, then fight or jump.
- Thargoids interdict too. Interdicting a clean ship is a crime.
- Update 8 added AI bounty hunters and assassins who chase players on faction missions and may interdict them.

### Docking

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Request range | 7.5 km, from the left-hand panel |
| Safe speed | Under 100 m/s |
| Pads | Small, medium or large; a numbered pad is assigned |
| Denial reasons | No space, too large, hostile, offences, too far, fighter still out, no reason given |
| Wanted or fined pilots | Dock anonymously |

- Line up 7–10 km out and fly into the slot on the green-light side.
- Station offences include blocking an airlock or pad, trespass, loitering, dumping cargo nearby, and firing or colliding in the no-fire zone.
- Docking computers can land the ship (see Ship modules), but guides still teach manual docking because auto-docking can fail.
- Stations can be under repair, damaged, abandoned or under attack.

### Planets, landing and SRVs

- Approaching a planet in supercruise enters Orbital Cruise, then Glide carries the ship down toward the surface.
- Altitude reads from the planet's mean radius high up and from the real surface low down.
- Guides keep new pilots off worlds above 1 g. Thin-atmosphere worlds are landable with Odyssey.
- The ship launches and recovers SRVs. From an SRV you can dismiss the ship or recall it.
- The Rhino SRV (2 September 2026) adds a planetary mineral scanner and deployable mining rigs.

### HUD modes and lights

- The HUD switches between combat mode and analysis mode, each with its own sight colours.
- Night vision and the Full Spectrum System scanner arrived with Beyond Chapter Four on 11 December 2018.
- Ship lights, night vision and the SRV high beam are separate toggles.
- The system map marks landable bodies. The radar turns red when the ship is too fast to scoop cargo.

### Combat missions

- Assassination and Takedown missions name a target. Since Update 8 some targets are flight risks: they flee when alarmed, and the mission fails if they reach hyperspace.
- Massacre missions ask for a kill count. Players stack them, reporting that missions from different issuers count the same kills.
- Wing missions (Beyond, 2018) share one objective across a wing.
- Update 8 added AI bounty hunters and assassins who hunt players on faction missions.

### Bounty hunting and piracy tools

- The Kill Warrant Scanner shows every bounty on a target and its full crime list. Since Update 7 it includes crimes by physical crew.
- The Frame Shift Wake Scanner follows a ship that jumped away.
- Scans reveal more in stages: first pilot name and rank; then shield and hull; then faction, legal status, bounty, the targeted subsystem and Power allegiance.
- Subsystem targeting cycles through a target's modules, so you can disable drives or a power plant instead of killing.
- Pirates shoot the shields down, then fire hatch-breaker limpets to pop cargo out. Other limpets collect cargo, transfer fuel, prospect, repair, research and decontaminate.

### Rewards

| Reward | Earned by |
| --- | --- |
| Bounty voucher | Destroying a wanted ship; one share per faction that posted a bounty |
| Combat bond | Kills in a conflict zone |
| Capital-ship bond | Capital-ship battles |
| On-foot combat bond | On-foot conflict zones |
| Trade, settlement and scan vouchers | Non-combat work |

- Flying solo, the last hit takes the bounty. In a wing, everyone who damaged the ship gets the voucher.
- Vouchers ride in your ship until cashed with the issuing faction, or through a broker for a cut (25% in the journal's example).
- Interstellar Factors, in low-security and anarchy systems, also pay off your own bounties.

### Fines, bounties and notoriety

- Minor offences such as smuggling and loitering bring fines. Destroying ships or SRVs, or killing people, brings bounties.
- Pay fines where they were issued: dock anonymously, then visit the security contact. Unpaid fines turn into bounties.
- Bounties are paid at Interstellar Factors, reached in your own ship, as crew or by Apex taxi.
- The ship used becomes a tool of crime and stays Wanted until its bounty clears. On-foot crimes away from the ship leave it clean.
- Notoriety rises with crime sprees; destroying one ship weighs like many on-foot killings. High notoriety brings system security and Advanced Tactical Response.
- Bounties cannot be paid while notoriety is above zero, so pilots wait it out, usually in an anarchy. Players report it falls one point per 2 hours of play.
- Legal states: Clean, Illegal Cargo, Speeding, Wanted, Hostile, Passenger Wanted, Warrant, Allied and Thargoid.
- Authorities scan for cargo, crimes, cabin passengers and data.
- Crime statistics count fines, bounties, the highest bounty, murders by victim (citizens, Omnipol, guards), thefts by kind, malware uploads, settlements shut down and turrets destroyed.

### Impound, rebuy and jurisdiction

- A ship used in crimes with uncleared bounties is impounded, and you pay for its release.
- Each fine and bounty belongs to the faction that issued it.
- Players report that destroying an enemy power's ships in your own power's systems brings no bounty, but elsewhere it is a crime.
- Insurance: a lost ship costs 5% of its value to rebuy. A pilot who cannot pay restarts in a Sidewinder or sells a stored ship to cover it.
- A multicrew member who commits a crime in a lawful session is kicked automatically.

### NPC pilots

- NPCs carry the player combat ranks, Harmless to Elite, shown when scanned.
- Players report that higher-ranked NPCs fly better ships and fight harder.
- The ship warns when you, your fighter or your mothership is under attack.

### Planned: Law & Disorder

- Law & Disorder, due in December 2026, is a crime rework. Coverage says it will "completely change the crime system" and make piracy, smuggling and banditry pay far more.
- Previewed rules ([StarZen, October 2026](https://www.starzen.space/t/elite-dangerous-details-december-law-disorder-crime-overhaul/40111)): a crime first creates an Unconfirmed Bounty. It becomes permanent only if authorities or Omnipol scan you, or someone reports you on foot; leaving first clears it.
- Authority response time and strength follow system security.
- The Kill Warrant Scanner and anonymous docking go. Passive scans show nearby ships, factions, Powers and unconfirmed bounties, and stations that detect a criminal refuse landing.
- Crimes fall into five severity tiers, from warnings and on-the-spot fines upward. Crime records attach to the commander.
- Inhabited anarchies become Criminal Systems with criminal factions and corrupt enforcers, and pirates can demand cargo from NPC ships.
- Attacks between rival Powers count as warfare. The Pilots' Federation escalates against repeat player-killers, up to Advanced Tactical Response ships.

### Markets and economies

- Each market line has buy and sell prices, the galactic mean price, stock and demand with brackets, and producer, consumer and rare flags.
- High-supply goods show green and cost below the galactic average; high-demand goods sell above it. A station's economy and government decide what it trades.
- Economies feed each other: Refinery economies depend on Extraction, and Military and High Tech lean on Refinery (Frontier's 2025 guide).
- The galaxy map's Trade Routes view draws goods flows as coloured arcs for systems you docked at in the last 24 hours. Trade data for a system can be bought, 100 CR in the journal's example.
- Trading raises the influence of the station's owning faction. Cargo is not insured, and mission cargo cannot be sold.
- Frontier's 2025 guide suggests the Hauler, Cobra Mk III, Type-6, Type-9 and Panther Clipper Mk II, which now outcarries the Type-9.

### Faction states and markets

| State | Market effect |
| --- | --- |
| Boom | Consumes more of everything; trade missions pay more; about three weeks |
| Bust | Lowers wealth and influence; trading does not help end it |
| Famine | Demand for basic food; food deliveries ease it |
| Outbreak | Medicine shortages; medicine deliveries end it |
| Lockdown | Security up, wealth down; checkpoints appear; bounty hunting shortens it |
| Civil Unrest | Caused by crime; lowers wealth and living standards; combat ends it |

- Wars, elections, expansion and retreat are under Factions, background simulation and Powerplay.

### Black markets and smuggling

- Each jurisdiction bans some goods. Personal weapons are legal in a dictatorship but not in a theocracy.
- Illegal and stolen goods sell only at black markets, which take a cut. Galaxy map filters find stations with one.
- Goods become stolen through piracy, or when you abandon a mission and its cargo is flagged.
- Authorities scan passing ships. A 2016 wiki says ships below 20% heat are much harder to scan, so smugglers run silent and vent with heat sinks.
- Anarchies have no security, so almost anything trades there.
- Smuggling cargo comes from smuggling missions, from wrecks and pirate victims, or from legal goods carried where they are banned.

## Mining

Ships mine planetary rings and asteroid belts with lasers, abrasion blasters, displacement missiles and seismic charges. Since September 2026 the Rhino SRV also mines planet surfaces, and ring type, reserve level and hotspots decide what you find.

| Rocky | Non-metallic resources | Indite, Gallite, Coltan, Uraninite, Lepidolite, Rutile, Bauxite, Cobalt |

### Hotspots

- Fire Detailed Surface Scanner probes at a ring in supercruise and hotspots appear, each named for one mineral. The journal's example ring held one Low Temperature Diamonds and one Alexandrite hotspot.
- Players report hotspots add no cores but raise the odds that a core holds the named mineral. Diamond hotspots also lift laser yields and add sub-surface deposits.
- A Tritium hotspot turns almost every deposit into Tritium, except cores (2024 players).
- Players mine where two or three hotspots overlap.

### Methods

| Method | Tool | How it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laser | Mining laser, Mining Lance, Long Range Mining Laser or Mining Volley Repeater | Fire at any asteroid to knock fragments loose. Collector limpets or the cargo scoop gather them, and the refinery turns them into tonnes. |
| Surface deposits | Abrasion Blaster | Shoot glowing deposits on the surface to break off chunks. |
| Sub-surface deposits | Sub-Surface Displacement Missile | The missile drills in; release the trigger while the bar is blue to free the deposit. |
| Core (deep core) | Seismic Charge Launcher, Abrasion Blaster, Pulse Wave Analyser | Crack a motherlode asteroid along its fissures, then collect the chunks. |
| Planet surface (2026) | Rhino SRV with mineral scanner, refinery and mining rigs | Rigs extract from deposits whose density varies, that replenish over time, and whose output depends on temperature. |

- **Cracking a core**: each fissure has low, medium or high resistance, and each charge is set to 1, 2 or 3 bars by holding the trigger. The ship shows a blue target band for the total: too little leaves the rock whole, too much destroys the valuables.
- Charges left alone detonate when their countdown ends.
- Only mining lasers yield engineering materials (2018–23 guide).
- Loadouts: laser (lasers, refinery, prospector and collector controllers); surface (abrasion blaster); sub-surface (displacement missiles); deep core (seismic launcher, abrasion blaster and a Pulse Wave Analyser); or a hybrid of all four.

### Dangers

- Pirates demand your cargo. If you carry none they scan you and leave.

## Exploration and exobiology

Explorers find bodies with a discovery pulse, resolve them with the Full Spectrum System scanner and map them with surface probes. Data sells at Universal Cartographics, and since Odyssey on-foot exobiology pays up to 20 M CR per species.

### Tools

| Tool | How it is fitted | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery Scanner | Built into every ship | One pulse, the honk, identifies every star and counts the bodies and signals |
| Full Spectrum System scanner (FSS) | Built in; used in supercruise at zero throttle | Tune a frequency spectrum to resolve planets, moons, belt clusters and signal sources |
| Detailed Surface Scanner (DSS) | Optional internal, class 1, 250,000 CR | Fires probes that map a planet, moon or ring; probes refill on their own and take no cargo |
| Composition scanner | Ship equipment | Analyses surface material and logs it to the Codex |
| Genetic Sampler | Artemis suit tool, on foot | Samples surface organisms |

- The FSS and DSS arrived with Beyond Chapter Four on 11 December 2018. Horizons (15 December 2015) had added landings on airless worlds and the SRV wave scanner.
- Support kit: fuel scoop, heat sink launcher, SRV hangar and Auto Field-Maintenance Units, which repair modules but not the hull or power plant.
- Dedicated explorers: the medium Zorgon Peterson Mandalay and the large Caspian Explorer, both from 2024 with Supercruise Overcharge drives.
- The route plotter handles economical routes up to 1,000 ly and fastest routes up to 20,000 ly. Jump boosts are under Flight and ship systems.

### FSS spectrum

| Band | Position | Typical sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weak | Left | Small sources such as emission and distress signals |
| High-density radio | — | Conflict zones, stations, megaships, beacons |
| Medium | Near centre | Belts and airless bodies left of centre; planets with atmospheres right of it |
| Strong | Right | Water giants and gas giants |

- Signal fields blink blue along the orbital plane, and a dashed target circle means the tuning is off. A stronger response means more signals of that kind.
- Resolved signals report their type, a threat level for unidentified sources, the owning faction and the time remaining.

### Surface mapping

- A map completes at 90% coverage, then the scanner fills in to 100%.
- Each body shows a probe efficiency target that varies with its mass. Meeting it earns a bonus, and probes that miss the body do not count.
- Probes aimed at the disc cover the near side; probes aimed just past the limb curve round to the far side.
- Mapping reveals biological, geological and human signals, and ring hotspots. Geological signals are fumaroles, gas vents, geysers, ice fumaroles, ice geysers and lava spouts.
- The biological count is the number of genera, not specimens. A blue overlay marks terrain where a chosen genus can grow.
- Upgrades: the Expanded Probe Scanning Radius blueprint, an engineered DSS from a human tech broker, and a merc-gear long-range DSS.

### Codex and first discoveries

- The Codex logs each new kind of object you find, with its region (one of 42), traits and coordinates.
- First Discovered By goes to the first commander to sell an unclaimed resolved body; First Mapped By to the first to sell its map. Both are decided at sale, not at scan.
- Community tools also read a first-footfall flag on each body.

### Selling data

- Universal Cartographics buys data at stations and fleet carriers, but not within 20 ly of where it was gathered. Unsold data is lost with the ship.
- Value depends on body class and mass, terraforming, resolved versus mapped, first discovery, first mapping and the efficiency bonus.
- Appearance, landability, materials, orbit and distance to the sale point do not change value.

| Term (community reverse-engineering) | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Star | k + mass × k ÷ 66.25; one third if only honked |
| Body after FSS scan | k + k × 0.56591828 × mass^0.2 |
| Terraformable body | Adds the class's terraform k |
| Mapping | × 10/3; × 3.6996 if also first discovered; × 8.0956 if first mapped only |
| Odyssey mapping bonus | + the larger of 30% or 555 CR |
| Efficiency bonus | × 1.25 on the mapped value |
| First discovery | × 2.6 |
| Floor | 500 CR per body |
| Whole system | + 1,000 CR per body fully scanned; + 10,000 CR per planet fully mapped |

- Worked example: a 1 Earth-mass Earth-like world is worth about 283,600 CR scanned, about 1.54 M CR mapped, and about 4.43 M CR first discovered and first mapped.

### Exobiology rules

- Sample three of a species with the Genetic Sampler, each at least the genus's colony range from the last, then move on.
- A DSS map shows how many genera live on a body and where each can grow. A ship or SRV shortens the walk between colonies.
- Samples sell at Vista Genomics, and unsold samples are lost with the ship.
- The first commander to log a species earns five times its base value, for example 95 M CR for Stratum Tectonicas.
- Exobiologist rank follows credits earned: 1.5 M CR for rank 1, roughly doubling to 520 M CR for Elite (2021 figures).

## Missions and activities

Minor factions post missions on each station's board, passengers book through a separate lounge, and Odyssey adds on-foot contracts. Signal sources, community goals and Operations, the instanced squad scenarios of 2026, fill in the rest.

### Where missions come from

- Mission Boards carry minor-faction work, the Passenger Lounge carries passenger contracts, and on-foot boards and contacts sit in stations and settlements. Operations launch from the ship panel while docked.
- The issuer, the target faction and the faction that gains influence can all differ. You choose one reward package: credits, reputation, influence, materials or commodities.
- What a board offers depends on faction state, location, platform and the current update.
- The Beginner Zone (23 April 2019) offers simpler Sidewinder-sized jobs in starter systems until your first rank, with a cheaper rebuy.

### Operations (2026)

- Operations are multi-stage instanced scenarios mixing ship and on-foot play, for squads of one to four commanders. NPCs never fill empty slots.
- An Operation Runner carries the squad in and acts as its spawn point. Difficulty is Easy or Hard, and Hard pays more.
- Mercenary Mode leaves Powerplay alone; Powerplay Mode undermines a chosen Power, never one a squad member serves.
- Rewards are credits, materials, Merc Coins and merits; Merc Coins buy modules and blueprints at existing vendors. Bounties are set aside on entry, and redeploying carries no death penalty.
- Operations start from starports, outposts, fleet or squadron carriers, or market planetports.

### Mission rules

- Route stacking runs several contracts on one trip, each with its own deadline, reward and influence effect.
- Massacre contracts from different issuers against the same faction share each kill, one contract per issuer at a time. You need close to the largest quota per issuer, not the sum.
- Only Team missions can be shared, and each commander must accept the share.
- Influence marks (+ to +++++) are relative strengths, scaled to system population and the day's activity, and land at the daily tick. Taking more influence costs part of the other rewards.
- Failing a mission can hurt the associated faction. A legal contract does not excuse the crimes you commit doing it.

## Factions, background simulation and Powerplay

Every populated system holds up to eight minor factions whose influence, states and conflicts update at a daily tick from what players do. Above that, Powerplay 2.0 (October 2024) lets commanders pledge to one of twelve Powers and fight weekly for system control.

### Minor factions and influence

- Faction influence in a system adds up to 100%. The faction with the most controls the system and sets its government.
- Influence, conflicts and states resolve at a roughly daily background simulation (BGS) tick, whose time drifts and varies by system.
- The tick runs in three phases: conflicts resolve, then states start or end, then the day's player activity is applied.
- Influence does not decay on its own, and small populations move faster: systems under 1 million people shift more easily than those over 1 billion.
- Each system has economy and security sliders that drift back to centre when nobody acts. Hostile reputation recovers a little each day until it reaches Unfriendly.
- The BGS is not mechanically tied to the superpowers.

### What moves influence

| Player action | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Completing missions | Raises the affected faction; any faction present can gain |
| Trading, including mined goods | Raises the controlling asset owner, most of all in Boom |
| Selling exploration data | Raises the controlling asset owner; nothing negative can counter it, and it pushes Expansion hardest |
| Selling exobiology data | Moves influence |
| Bounty vouchers | Raise influence and push the security slider up |
| Combat bonds and conflict-zone wins | Help a side in a war or civil war |
| Odyssey settlement work | Moves influence |
| Missions for rivals | Lower the others, since influence is zero-sum |
| Killing a faction's ships in its own space | Lowers that faction |
| Smuggling | Lowers influence |
| Murder and illegal missions | Push the security slider down |
| Failed missions and crime | Can hurt the associated faction |

### Conflicts, expansion and retreat

- Conflicts start when two factions' influence is close, and freeze influence between them while they run.
- Each day goes to the side that wins more conflict zones and hands in more combat bonds, or, in elections, more election missions and economic influence.
- A conflict lasted up to 7 days in 2019, the side with more days won taking it; a draw ends in ceasefire. Winning takes an asset, and taking the largest asset takes the system.
- Wars need at least one non-native faction; civil wars are between two native ones.

| Ethos pairing | Anarchy | Autocrats | Corporations | Social |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anarchy | War | War | War | War |
| Autocrats | War | Election | War | War |
| Corporations | War | War | Election | War |
| Social | War | War | War | Election |

- Expansion can start above about 75% influence and searches within 20 ly. About 15% influence is taken as an expansion tax, and the origin loses wealth and development.
- A non-native faction below about 2.5% enters Retreat for about 5 days and leaves if it does not recover. Its influence is shared among the rest.
- A player minor faction is a squadron's in-game faction; it starts with no assets and must win conflicts to gain them. A 2024 guide says Frontier had stopped taking new requests.

### Merits and system control

- Merits are earned at once, never decay and cannot be spent. Almost anything earns them: rare trade, pirate bounties, hacking holo-ads with a recon limpet, settlement data, and killing rival Powers' commanders.
- Each Power gives acquisition, reinforcement and undermining one of four approaches (Finance, Social, Combat, Covert). Since April 2025 the local activities are set server-side.

| System state | Category | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unoccupied | Acquisition | No Power controls it |
| Expansion | Acquisition | A Power is trying to take it |
| Contested | Acquisition | Two or more Powers compete |
| Exploited | Control | Lowest control; must lie within 20 ly of a Fortified or Stronghold system |
| Fortified | Control | High control; anchors a 20 ly bubble |
| Stronghold | Control | Highest control; a Stronghold Carrier may offer pledged commanders better markets |

- States settle each weekly cycle; a Contested system goes to the Power with the highest control score. Exploited systems outside a bubble can fall back to Unoccupied.

- In a Stronghold, ships may be attacked when scanned or entering a starport; heat sinks and silent running avoid it.
- Leaderboards reset each cycle, paying credits and showing the top players' portraits as badges.
- Merit multipliers since Trailblazers Update 3.4 (2025): undermining +15%, acquisition 0%, reinforcement −35% plus a sliding −20% to +30% set by the last day's undermining.

### How interceptors fight

- **Main cannon**: bursts at about 3 km with heavy hull and module damage; running cold in orbit evades it.
- **Hearts**: kill them one by one, exposed by Guardian Gauss fire; each heart rebuilds the drone swarm if it still lives.
- **Lightning pulse** after a heart dies: within 800 m it strips shields, adds drag, caps speed, reboots modules and scrambles the HUD.
- **Caustic missiles**: corrosion that keeps damaging the hull until you run hot or use a decontamination limpet.
- **Energy surge (shutdown field)**: disables ships within 10 km for about 30 s; countered by a Shutdown Field Neutraliser or by running silent beyond 3 km.
- **Enrage timer**: when it runs out, the whole swarm turns into suicide missiles.

### Settlements and security

- Settlement economies include agricultural, extraction, high-tech, industrial, military and tourism; a community mapping tool counts 28 settlement types.
- A settlement follows its controlling faction's security rules, not the system's. Anarchy settlements give no fines, bounties or notoriety, even for murder.
- Doors need an authority level: clone a cleared NPC's profile with the Profile Analyser, or cut in with the Arc Cutter. Some areas are restricted.
- Alarms bring turrets and skimmer drones; anti-ship turrets can be shut off from a console inside. A data-port alarm covers only the current room.
- Security NPCs scan you for crimes; since December 2025 the scan reaches further and the NPC turns to face you first.
- HAB buildings hold data ports and lockers of assets. Leaving to supercruise and coming back resets most loot.

### Squadrons and squadron carriers

- Squadrons arrived with Beyond Chapter 4 (December 2018) and were overhauled by Vanguards (19 August 2025).
- Up to 600 members (raised from 500 on 22 August 2025) and 25 pending applications.
- Up to 15 ranks, each with its own permissions: jumping the carrier, setting market orders, viewing or using the bank.
- Identity: logo, motto, banners and three-layer decals shown on members' ships.
- A squadron aligns with a minor faction. Its primary perk is always on; a secondary perk works where that faction controls the system. Named perks: Increased Team Rewards, Increased Colonisation Commodities Profit, Cargo Insurance.

- Squadron feed, bulletin board, member statuses, and a browser that can flag new-player-friendly squadrons.
- **Squadron carrier** (Javelin-class): bigger than a personal carrier and bought by leaders. It has a Briefing Room, a shared Treasury for credits and a Squadron Bank for materials, commodities, items and whole ships (not engineered modules or Jump Start ships). Its price and specs were not found.

## Ideas for Oolite

These are Elite Dangerous systems that could fit Oolite's single-player, scriptable galaxy, each paired with what Oolite's core already has. Several build on existing pieces, such as contract reputation, the torus drive's mass lock and per-commodity legality.

### Five that fit best

1. **Rare goods tied to named systems.** Elite Dangerous already sells Lavian Brandy, Leestian Evil Juice, Diso Ma Corn and Zaonce's Leathery Eggs, all from Oolite's Galaxy 1 namesakes.
2. **System states that move prices and missions.** Boom, Famine, Outbreak, Lockdown and War would layer change onto Oolite's fixed governments and economies.
3. **Signal sources with threat levels.** They give torus-drive cruising something to stop for, and fit the populator's existing roles.
4. **Cartography data and first-discovery tags.** Exploration would become a third career beside trading and combat.
5. **A telemetry feed for companion apps.** The component tests already drive the game over the debug console, so game state is reachable from outside.
